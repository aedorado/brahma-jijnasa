## q1 | single-choice | easy

**Q:** Why did Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura establish 64 Gauḍīya Maṭha centers and emphasize foundational principles of community cooperation?

**Options:**
- [ ] To build political influence and challenge secular legislative authorities across India
- [x] To create coordinated spiritual embassies where devotees unitedly perform saṅkīrtana and serve the Lord
- [ ] To centralize personal financial assets under the sole ownership of individual temple managers
- [ ] To withdraw completely from urban society into isolated monastic retirement

**Ref:** Śrī Bhaktisiddhānta Vaibhava, Vol. 1, Ch. 3

**Explanation:** Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura established 64 Gauḍīya Maṭha branches as spiritual embassies of Śrī Caitanya Maṭha to enable devotees to cooperatively perform saṅkīrtana and spread Lord Caitanya's message without material motives.

## q2 | multiple-select | easy

**Q:** According to the discourses of Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, which qualities are vital for sustaining long-term harmony within a Vaiṣṇava community? (Select all that apply)

**Options:**
- [x] Viewing oneself as a humble servant of all Vaiṣṇavas without seeking personal prestige (pratiṣṭhā)
- [ ] Aggressively monitoring the flaws and minor personal shortcomings of peer devotees
- [x] Mutual cooperation in kīrtana-yajña, recognizing that alone one possesses no spiritual strength
- [x] Demonstrating genuine compassion (jīve dayā) and patience toward struggling practitioners

**Ref:** Śrī Bhaktisiddhānta Vaibhava, Vol. 2; 1927 & 1931 Addresses

**Explanation:** Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta taught that freedom from desire for fame (pratiṣṭhā), collaborative kīrtana-yajña ('alone I have no strength'), and heartfelt compassion sustain community harmony. Fault-finding (para-carcā) ruins spiritual communities.

## q3 | true-false | easy

**Q:** True or False: Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura taught that temple administrators and managers must never consider themselves proprietors or lords over temple property, but rather humble caretakers serving the servants of the Lord.

**Options:**
- [x] True
- [ ] False

**Ref:** Śrī Bhaktisiddhānta Vaibhava, Vol. 2

**Explanation:** Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta taught that a true guru or manager is 'heavy' (guru) only by not taking personal possession of Kṛṣṇa's property, serving strictly as the servant of the servants. Those taking possession become light and fallible.

## q8 | assertion-reason | easy

**Assertion:** Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura equipped his preachers with motor cars, printing presses, and Western-tailored coats rather than embracing dry asceticism.

**Reason:** Under the principle of yukta-vairāgya formulated by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, any material facility can and should be engaged 100% in the service of Lord Kṛṣṇa.

**Q:** Evaluate the relationship between the following Assertion and Reason regarding community resources:

**Options:**
- [x] Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
- [ ] Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion.
- [ ] Assertion is true, but Reason is false.
- [ ] Assertion is false, but Reason is true.

**Ref:** Śrī Bhaktisiddhānta Vaibhava, Vol. 1, Ch. 3; Ācārya Kesarī

**Explanation:** Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta rejected phalgū-vairāgya (dry, artificial renunciation) and championed yukta-vairāgya, demonstrating that modern facilities like automobiles and high-speed presses belong to Kṛṣṇa and must be used to spread His glories.

## q13 | two-truths-one-false | easy

**Q:** Examine these three statements regarding Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura's instructions on āśrama discipline. Select the statement that is FALSE:

**Options:**
- [ ] He taught that associating with worldly people looking for faults is far more detrimental than physical disease.
- [x] He encouraged devotees to openly publicize and criticize the personal shortcomings of peers during morning classes.
- [ ] He instructed that when minor friction occurs between devotees, they should tolerate it and discourse exclusively about Lord Hari.

**Ref:** Śrī Bhaktisiddhānta Vaibhava, Vol. 2, 1931 Address

**Explanation:** The second statement is false. Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta strictly condemned criticizing others (para-carcā), citing the warning 'para-carcākera gati nāhi kona kāle' and instructing disciples to focus exclusively on correcting their own flaws.

## q14 | case-study | easy

**Scenario:** Field preachers and resident managers come into administrative friction regarding allocation of collection and temple resources.

**Q:** Case Study: In a Gauḍīya Maṭha branch, an energetic traveling preaching party returns to the temple and clashes with the temple manager over kitchen resources and travel finances. Both parties appeal to Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura. Based on his historical resolutions, how did he handle such disputes?

**Options:**
- [ ] He abolished the traveling preaching party and ordered all members to remain permanently inside the temple compound.
- [ ] He ousted the temple manager and gave full, unaccountable financial authority to the traveling preachers.
- [x] He reminded both sides that internal management and external outreach are equal limbs of Śrī Caitanya's mission, demanding mutual respect, shared sacrifice, and strict accountability to Hari.
- [ ] He encouraged competition between them, declaring that whichever party raised the most funds would govern the temple.

**Ref:** Śrī Bhaktisiddhānta Vaibhava, Vol. 2, Ch. 4; Ācārya Kesarī

**Explanation:** In his guidance to managers and preachers, Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta emphasized that both temple worship (internal maintenance) and book distribution/preaching (external outreach) are interdependent limbs of one mission, requiring mutual honor and cooperation under Kṛṣṇa.
