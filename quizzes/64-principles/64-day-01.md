## q1 | single-choice | easy

**Q:** Why is the sacred month of Kārtika celebrated as the foremost month of the entire year?

**Options:**
- [ ] Observances during this month can only be undertaken by renounced hermits living in forest hermitages
- [ ] The spiritual merit of vows is granted only if accompanied by elaborate golden donations to royalty
- [x] Lord Kṛṣṇa generously multiplies the spiritual results of even the smallest loving offering
- [ ] Devotional activities bear fruit exclusively when performed during auspicious planetary alignments

**Ref:** Hari-bhakti-vilāsa 16.41-42

**Explanation:** Hari-bhakti-vilāsa explains that just as Lord Dāmodara is naturally affectionate toward His devotees, the sacred month of Kārtika awards unlimited spiritual merit for even the simplest humble offering of devotion.

**Options:**
- [x] Offering illuminated ghee or sesame lamps before Lord Dāmodara (dīpa-dāna)
- [ ] Performing severe multi-day waterless fasts in solitary mountain caves
- [x] Bathing reverently before sunrise in a sacred river or tīrtha (prātaḥ-snāna)
- [x] Daily watering, circumambulating, and serving the sacred Tulasī plant (tulasī-sevana)

**Explanation:** Hari-bhakti-vilāsa highlights five essential limbs for Kārtika: early morning bathing, offering lamps, serving Tulasī, keeping night vigils (jāgaraṇa), and concluding the vow (udyāpana).

- His eyes flooded with tears and His chest trembled with soft sobs as Mother Yaśodā caught Him from behind.
- Although the universe resides within His belly, He allowed Himself to be bound to a wooden mortar by pure motherly love.

**Q:** Identify the divine personality described by these scriptural clues:

**Options:**
- [ ] Lord Vāmanadeva
- [ ] Lord Rāmacandra

**Explanation:** Lord Dāmodara is the sweet childhood form of Lord Kṛṣṇa who allowed His waist to be bound (dāma-udara) by Mother Yaśodā's maternal affection.

## q6 | sequence | easy

**Q:** Arrange these sequential milestones of the Dāmodara pastime in their proper chronological order:

**Items:**
- [3] Mother Yaśodā endeavors tirelessly to bind her restless son to the wooden mortar with household churning cords
- [1] Lord Kṛṣṇa breaks the yogurt pot with a stone grinder and distributes churned butter to the forest monkeys
- [4] Lord Kṛṣṇa crawls between the twin arjuna trees, pulling down the heavy trunks and liberating Kuvera's sons
- [2] Mother Yaśodā pursues the fleeing child, gently captures Him from behind, and puts down her stick

**Ref:** Hari-bhakti-vilāsa 16.199-206 & Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.9-10

**Explanation:** The pastime moves from Lord Kṛṣṇa breaking the yogurt pot, to Mother Yaśodā chasing and catching Him, tying Him to the mortar, and Kṛṣṇa dragging the mortar to liberate Nalakūvara and Maṇigrīva.

**Options:**

- [x] Lord Kṛṣṇa's compassionate grace descending upon His devotee (kṛpā)

## q13 | single-choice | easy

**Q:** In the Dāmodara-līlā, what spiritual truth is revealed by the rope continually remaining two fingers too short?

**Options:**
- [ ] Ordinary household ropes possess insufficient physical strength to hold the Lord
- [x] Human endeavor alone cannot attain the Lord without His unconditional mercy
- [ ] The child's body was expanding through mystic yoga-māyā to escape maternal discipline
- [ ] Mother Yaśodā needed to perform formal Vedic rites before completing household vows

**Ref:** Hari-bhakti-vilāsa 16.200 commentary & Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.9.15-18

**Explanation:** The two-finger gap signifies that spiritual perfection requires two inseparable components: the devotee's sincere, persistent effort and Lord Kṛṣṇa's descending causeless mercy.
